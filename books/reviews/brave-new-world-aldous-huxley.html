Brave New World

by Aldous Huxley
★★★★★ 5/5 (avg 4.00)
HarperPerennial / Perennial Classics • 1998 • 268 pages
Brave New World is philosophic horror story, which seems(to some people) realistic view of future and thus the horror is produced if that which is described in the book is morally bad to those people who think in that way. Part of this book is currently true like those short relationships(they're called one night-stands) and but that is only minor future prediction of Huxley's. So collectively this book hasn't come true, but IMO modern world is really hedonistic and probably only gets more hedonistic in time. In next chapter I will explain hedonism in modern context.<br/><br/><b>What is hedonism?</b><br/><br/>Hedonism is the easy path(totally cliché saying sorry) which requires no thinking and takes you out of the suffering of modern world(any world). But this hedonism doesn't lead anybody anywhere it only makes the person more miserable and easier to be pulled back to the hedonistic pleasures. This loop will continue ad infinitum(again and again forever) while person finds different kind of hedonistic pleasures since they have ran out of enjoyment in one pleasure. But many times this pleasure is raw kind of pleasure(watching TV, Netflix, video games, reading trash, scrolling black screen of Instagram or Tiktok), which is not controlled by you, but your brain's automatic pathways. But you say: "I really like TV, Instagram(I also meet friends here) or whatever." Indeed it's not your fault(if you want to fix it, make it your fault), but the culture of consumerism. Escaping this media black box mindset prison is not easy, since it entails giving up some consumerism propaganda and propably some of your "friends". But what kind of friend only cares how you show your lifestyle at Instagram or Facebook? Consumerist friend(almost sounds like a joke, but it's kinda true I think)! All the productivity books ever say that you are the average of your friends, so the only way of sinking consumerism IMO is quiting all of these media sites or rather putting healthy limits to them(reward yourself for keeping boundaries), but ultimately if you don't get paid for using media or get any better at something then it's probably very bad way to spend time. My name is Vincent, will you care if I were to die? The same is true on the other direction also, since we don't have real relationship sadly. So the lesson hear is that anybody you meet online will never care if you life or die and that is not their fault, but just a fact that cannot(atleast currently) be fixed using internet(probably some VR set in future will make this kinda possible, which is scary IMO(in some ways atleast)). Next chapter I will explain how to life with consumerism and healthly with some hedonistic aspects of modern society. <br/><br/><b>How to life a good life in 21st century?</b><br/><br/>I don't know about you, but in my experience we get more satisfaction from activities, that are challenging(not too much and not too little) and that either grow our character or our minds(or better both). So spending freetime exercising might be really good way of spending time, but here comes the tricky part. We never fully get rid of consumerism. Indeed reading this book is consumerism(IMO minimalist consumerism is good not anti-capitalist here). This consumerism should be mainly left behind, but since we people still have those urges here are some ground rules to control your life(tiktok ticking away every sentence you read(your life I mean, killing you more with every single word(hopefully more wise words that you usually read and thus I wanted to write this))). Ground rules for consumerism:<br/><br/>1. Cut rather than add(TV shows, books, podcasts)(I myself have problems with this, but I try to add things I myself enjoy rather than what other people enjoy and thus it's less consumerism and mor self-fulfillment). <br/><br/>2. Get into habit of cutting content out of your life using harsh questions(it's not easy to destroy using only question: "do I need this?", which is fine if you have great character, but what works better is: "Should I die 40 minutes of watching Ancient Aliens TV series(consumerism at it's best(or rather worst)) activity?")<br/><br/>3. Start making absolute judgements about where to spend your time(energy could be used as synonym). Spending time is black and white thinking, since there's always that opportunity cost(meaning you cannot do many things at same time). When you are planning, then you can think many ways, but when it comes down to it. You can only make one thing at one time or audiobook while running(which is big expection). <br/><br/>4. Listening is your best friend, since you can listen while doing boring chores and thus get the consumerist urgest under control. <br/><br/>5. Spending time with friends should be not multitasking and a friend that looks phone while being with you is not real friend, but rather a consumerist puppet(hedonistic probably, which is not your fault, but there are people who really listen to you without needing to type messages to somebody else(these people want to be somewhere else and not with person in front. So how can these anybody's friend?)). <br/><br/>Those are the main rules I can come up. Next chapter is about achieving goals.<br/><br/><br/>Let's continue towards achieving something. First of all. Why should you try to achieve anything and what that anything should be? Achieving is cliche, but under cliche lies some truth(more truth than blindless consumerism(consuming everything(I've been there, just look at my Goodreads account, which is also starting to be under my control))). Back to the point. Achieving awakens soul from inertia, which comes from staying too long at same point. I don't know why this happens, but it happens after you really achieve something, so while cannot find any philosophical justification we just must accept this as truth that comes from experience. Basically awakening from inertia means that you grow more confident and feeling better at your own skin. If your achiement is too easy, then you will not feel this same feeling, it must be challenging in some way in order to feel this(studying something boring, exercising past endurance or just beating procastination and after that studying something boring). This must be challenging, since how can you feel like you're moving forward as a person, if you cannot proof yourself, that you can do better? This proof that you can do better is ultimately what makes person happy. Just think about when you felt most bad about yourself: When you were partying with your friends and drinking alcohol? Or doing something despite not wanting to? The latter is ultimately what makes person happy, since it proves their virtue to themselves. Prooving virtue is good life while not prooving this is ironically the more unhappy and miserable one even though prooving virtue is harder(in some ways). <br/><br/><br/><b>How to improve virtue?</b><br/><br/>Virtue should be side goal of doing something. Virtue cannot fully be gotten from books, but some books are good companions to have. No not talking about some trash self-help books(some classics are fine), but more rigorous philosophy books. I recommend books from Seneca, Shopenhauer, Epikuros(to understand hedonism), Aristoteles and Socrates. You do not need to read all of the books, mainly books that describe good living and thus help you to reach your own conclusions and awaken those sleeping brainpathways. These philosophers will give rigour to your thinking, which will go far, but after and during time of philosophical training(200h is enough(read books take notes and discuss with them about 10+ books(2 from each philosopher))) you should have some goal to awaken you from the inertia of being too long only yourself and not adding proof of virtue to you for long time. Reading philosophers is kinda hard goal, but once you get soul out of that box of inertia, it will not feel like anything. But start with something, like downloading some anti action audiobook or something(be creative, but remember that consumerism is always right around corner getting you into that rabbit hole of just consuming little bit more and not anything of value, like Seneca said: "Everything is nowhere" and thus just find something that is valuable and but it on loop rather than always trying to find better information).<br/><br/>Best of wishes from Vincent, hopefully this helps you.
