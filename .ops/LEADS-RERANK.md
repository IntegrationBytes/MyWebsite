# RE-RANK OF `/home/user/MyWebsite/.ops/leads.csv` AGAINST THE REVENUE OFFER

## The scoring change, stated first

The old test asked one question about the **prospect's own back office**: *do they have 2–3 FTE on one repetitive workflow?* The new test asks five questions about the **prospect's customers**:

| | New qualifying question | Why it decides the sale |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Do they sell to **companies**, with an ICP nameable in one sentence? | If the buyer is a consumer or a taloyhtiö, there is no company to research and no signal to find. |
| Q2 | Does their target market **emit dated public events**? | The Signal Run is only as good as the signal density in the buyer's market. No public events = no product. |
| Q3 | Is there a **named person who owns growth, sales or M&A**? | Cost-side sells to a CEO/CFO. Revenue-side sells to whoever carries a number. Different人, different email. |
| Q4 | Is one extra won customer worth **> €10k**? | €3,500 must be repaid by a fraction of one deal, or the maths is a debate. |
| Q5 | Is their market **consolidating**, and are they an acquirer? | The highest-value use of this product is not customer leads — it is **acquisition-target origination**. |

**Q5 is the discovery in this dataset.** Eight of the 32 are active acquirers with public, numeric growth mandates. For them the deliverable is not "30 prospects," it is "30 acquisition candidates with owner age, revenue, succession status and no successor named." One completed acquisition is worth €1M+ in acquired revenue, which makes a €3,500 file a rounding error and makes the Density Check unnecessary — an acquirer buying ten companies over two years will re-buy the file quarterly forever.

**A second rule fell out of the data and it is load-bearing:** the buyer's own ICP must be **Finland-resident companies**. Vincent's proof artifact was built entirely on Finnish sources — Asiakastieto, Finder, Proff, kaupparekisteri, Yle, regional press, Finnish job boards. Selling a Signal Run on global marine/mining OEMs or the Norwegian market is a product he has not run once. That single rule demotes three otherwise-excellent leads (ATA Gears, Hydroline, Eilakaisla) out of Tier A.

Also confirmed: **31 of 32 `est_deal_size` values are cost-side** (`"EUR 35-50k build + EUR 3-4k/month AgentOps"`). Only Balanco carries a revenue-side value (`"EUR 7,500 Buy-Box; recurring Deal Flow thereafter"`). The whole column is now wrong and must be rewritten before the file is shown to anyone — it currently prices the abandoned offer, in the showcase artifact, in writing.

## (d) THE IDEAL NEW LEAD — spec for a fresh search

Three ICPs. They are not variants; they buy different rungs, so the pipeline should be run as three separate searches with separate acceptance gates.

### ICP-1 — "The Funded Acquirer" (highest value, buys Rung 2 then re-buys quarterly)

**Firmographics**
- Finland-domiciled, €5M–€60M revenue, 40–550 staff
- Has closed **≥2 acquisitions in the last 24 months**, at least one within 12 months
- Institutional capital present: a Finnish mid-market PE holder (Vaaka, Voland, Korona Invest, Intera, Sponsor, Bocap, Juuri, Panostaja, Lifeline, Wetera), or a stated professionalisation step such as first independent board members
- Operates in a **fragmented, owner-operated sector** — tilitoimisto, isännöinti, sähkö/LVI-urakointi, 3PL/huolinta, hoiva, IT-palvelut

**Required signal (≥1, dated, ≤180 days)**
- A published numeric growth target with a deadline (Balanco: €35M by 2027)
- A closed acquisition where the acquired firm keeps its own name and organisation
- A named person hired or promoted into an M&A / growth / development role
- A PE investment with a stated market-leadership mandate

**Required person:** a title containing *yrityskauppa, kasvu, M&A, liiketoiminnan kehitys, konsernijohtaja,* or a founder-CEO who signed the last deal personally. **Not** a CFO or an IT manager.

**Deliverable pitched:** not customer leads — a target list. 30 acquisition candidates in their sector with revenue, headcount, owner age, ownership concentration, no named successor, and a dated succession or fatigue signal.

**Why it wins:** the ROI argument is over in one sentence, and the buy-box does not deplete, so the Density Check passes automatically.

Everywhere else, acquisitions and factory investments happen once or twice a year per company, month 1 harvests a backlog and month 3 collapses. Here the signal is continuous. If the €2,400/month Standing Run is going to have any customers at all, they come from ICP-3.

### Hard disqualifiers — apply before any enrichment spend

1. **Sells to consumers or to taloyhtiöt/housing companies.** Their buying decisions are board votes at yhtiökokous and are invisible publicly. Kills 4 rows in the current file.
2. **Demand-constrained, not demand-seeking.** If a named executive has publicly said capacity or labour is the bottleneck, they will not buy leads. Screen for the phrase pattern *pullonkaula / kapasiteetti / emme ehdi / rekrytointihaaste*. Kills Feon and Finnglass.
3. **Acquired within the last 12 months.** Buying authority has moved to the parent; pitch the parent. Kills 3PLogistiikka.
4. **>250 staff or >€100M revenue.** Procurement, legal review, a 9-month cycle, and a 22-year-old with one paid engagement will not clear it.
5. **Runs an internal AI team.** They build. Kills DialOk.
6. **Their own ICP is not Finland-resident.** Non-negotiable until at least one Finnish delivery is banked, because it is the difference between shipping the proven pipeline and improvising. Demotes ATA Gears, Hydroline, and Eilakaisla's Norway play.
7. **Overlaps a Wannado service line** — GEO, digital marketing, outsourced customer service, outsourced marketing management. Route, do not compete.
8. **No named person with a growth, sales or M&A title reachable at a published address.** A CEO-only, phone-only, info@ route is a Tier C row at best.
