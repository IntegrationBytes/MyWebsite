The finding that matters most

Your payback maths was wrong, in the direction that flattered the offer.

Building the calculator forced the numbers to compute, and the retainer turned out to break them. The obvious formula divides first-year cost by monthly saving. But the retainer recurs for as long as the agents run, so it has to come off the monthly saving before the build starts repaying: build ÷ (monthly saving − retainer).

€113,400a 4-person, 15h/week invoice workflow, per year
17 mopayback the naive formula reports
28 moactual payback at a €3k retainer
neverat a €6k retainer, the retainer eats the entire saving
The old guarantee would have fired on deals that went well. The site promised "I keep refunding the retainer until it does" — uncapped, with no defined metric, no named measurer and no end date. The new terms cap it at the lesser of 25% of the build fee and €12,000, in cash, one claim, sole remedy, against a baseline signed before the build is priced. I checked the new clause numerically: it cannot fire on a good deal, because "Build Fee Paid" excludes AgentOps fees.
The real qualifying question is not company size. It is whether they have 2–3 full-time-equivalent people on one repetitive workflow.
